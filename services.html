Services

The places an operation comes apart.

Each service is a place we analyze, review, and align products and processes, so the flow is smoother without adding headcount. Most engagements use more than one, because a split in one place is usually visible in another.

  1. 01

    Operating picture

    One view of the work in flight: what is open, who has it, and which commitment is at risk. Leadership and the floor read the same picture.

    Work in flight · Who has it · The commitment at risk

  2. 02

    Ownership

    A name on every duty that can stall the week, including the handoffs that currently belong to no one. Decisions that sit between groups get an owner.

    Named owners · Handoffs · Decisions between groups

  3. 03

    Cadence

    The meetings, reviews, and escalations that run the week, reduced to the ones that change the work. A calendar full of updates is not a cadence.

    Meetings · Reviews · Escalations

  4. 04

    Systems

    The tools, access, and records that must agree, including the software already in use and what it costs. AI is aligned to steps people already do, so it smooths the flow without a team hired to watch it. Compliance evidence, including SOC 2 and PCI, has to match those records. We join what the operation already uses. A replacement is recommended only when the current record cannot carry the work.

    Tools · Access · Records · AI · SOC 2 · PCI

  5. 05

    Sites

    The same standard at every location that does the work, with room for what is truly local. A second site does not get to invent a second operation.

    Every location · The same standard · What is truly local

  6. 06

    Transition

    After a merger, an acquisition, a leadership change, or a platform cutover, the operation needs one way of working, one set of access, and controls that match the work, before the old ways harden into two companies again.

    Merger · Acquisition · Leadership change · Platform cutover
