Cloud cost and reliability

The bill and the outage come from the same gap.

Cloud and SaaS spend and reliability come apart in the same places: a platform nobody owns, a second tool that records the same job, and access that outlives the person who needed it. Coalesce Ops reviews the software you already pay for and the platforms you already run.

What we will not promise

We will not quote a percentage you should expect to save. Anyone who opens with a savings number, before they have seen the seats and the path of the work, is guessing. The review names specific waste. It does not promise a ratio.

Bring the last invoice you trust and the list of people who can administer the account. If those two cannot sit on the same table, that is the start of the review, and it is enough to scope a first visit.

Cost, as an operator

  • Seats and subscriptions that do not match the people who still work there.
  • Two products that record the same work. One of them is usually the one a team is afraid to turn off.
  • A vendor holding data, or able to change a system, with no named owner.
  • A bill leadership cannot tie to a system the floor still uses.
  • Onboarding and offboarding that are still manual, so access and cost drift every time someone joins or leaves.

This is SaaS governance and vendor cost, which is already in the practice. The systems and cost account on the practice page is the short version. The point of the longer pass is to tie each line on the invoice to a job the operation still does.

A seat for someone who left is also an access finding. Treat it as both. Turning off the seat without knowing which job it still carries is how a “savings” becomes an outage the next morning.

Reliability without a new team

Reliability here means the people you have can tell what is down, who owns it, and what the customer was promised. It is not a rotation staffed by adding headcount. If the only way a platform stays up is a person you have not hired yet, the products and the processes are not aligned.

For each platform the week depends on, write three lines: who notices it is down, who is allowed to change it, and where the record of that change lives. Shared admin access with no owner is a cost problem, a reliability problem, and an access problem. That is why security and compliance sit in the same account. See SecOps consulting when the control is the thing that is drifting.

If the three lines are “we would call a vendor, on a shared login, and the record is a chat thread,” you do not yet have a reliability practice. You have a phone number. The engagement writes the owner and the record. It does not install a new on-call roster of people you do not have.

After a cutover

A platform cutover is in scope, before the old way and the new way harden into two operations. The infrastructure assessment is the wider pass across identity, network, and the platforms you already run. This page is the pass that starts from the invoice and from what happens when something fails.

Transition covers the same moment when the cause is a merger, an acquisition, or a leadership change. Say which one it is in the first note so the visit can be scoped to that week, not to a general tour of the cloud.
